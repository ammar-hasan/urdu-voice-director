# Dialogue performance

Use direction to reveal the playable action already latent in the scene, not to add a new performance on top of it.

## Direct intention, not decoration

Weak:

```text
[very emotionally]
تم نے مجھے بتایا کیوں نہیں؟
```

Playable:

```text
[hurt, trying not to accuse]
تم نے مجھے بتایا کیوں نہیں؟
```

The second cue gives the actor or model a tension to play. Use an audible action where possible:

- trying not to wake someone;
- hiding embarrassment;
- testing whether the other person is serious;
- keeping anger under control;
- explaining without humiliating;
- asking after a new realization.

## Direction ladder

Use the least intrusive layer that works:

1. **Wording and sequence** — preferred.
2. **Punctuation and turn break** — readable rhythm.
3. **Short direction cue** — only for material ambiguity or transition.
4. **Provider control** — only when verified and requested.
5. **Audio iteration** — required when the provider’s response is uncertain.

## Audible versus visible direction

`[whispering urgently]` is audible and may be suitable for a supported audio tag.

`[looking over his shoulder]` is visible. It may help a voice actor understand the scene, but an inline TTS system might read it or ignore it. Translate its audible consequence when justified:

```text
Rehearsal note: He checks whether anyone is nearby.
Audible cue: [quietly, cautious]
```

Do not pretend the audible cue fully replaces the physical action.

## Placement

Put a cue next to the phrase it governs:

```text
احمد: [defensive, hushed] کسی نے مجھے دیکھا ہی نہیں۔

احمد: اوہ... [the defense drops; sincere] یعنی پھر بھی غلط ہے؟
```

Avoid a single opening cue for a turn whose intention changes halfway through.

## Useful cue vocabulary

Choose specific combinations sparingly:

- `[آہستہ، مگر جلدی میں]`
- `[دبی ہوئی ناراضی]`
- `[شرمندگی چھپاتے ہوئے]`
- `[نرمی سے، بات واضح کرتے ہوئے]`
- `[soft realization]`
- `[quietly, trying to sound certain]`
- `[brief reflective pause]`
- `[cuts in before he can finish]`

Avoid:

- unsupported inner biography;
- camera direction;
- long literary sentences inside brackets;
- stacked synonyms such as `[softly, gently, warmly, lovingly]`;
- repeated `[softly]` on consecutive turns.

## Intensity calibration

Build intensity from the stakes and relationship:

- Suppressed anger often narrows pitch range, shortens phrasing, or hardens consonants; it does not require shouting.
- Fear may produce vigilance, clipped breath, concealment, or forced normality; trembling is only one option.
- Warm correction may remain firm; warmth does not erase seriousness.
- Embarrassment may accelerate, trail off, self-correct, joke, or become overly formal.

These are performance hypotheses, not universal acoustic rules. Validate with listening.

## Restraint test

Remove each cue in turn. If wording, punctuation, and context still produce the intended reading reliably, omit the cue. Keep cues that disambiguate the scene, mark a meaningful beat, or map to an actual provider control.
