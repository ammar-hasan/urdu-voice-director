# Poetry and Recitation

Load this reference for ghazal, nazm, qasida, marsiya, hamd, naat, manqabat, geet, rubai, bait-bazi, or any verse performed quietly, publicly, or in a mushaira. General dramatic direction is not enough: the poem’s metrical and sonic structure constrains the reading.

## First identify the text and performance

Record what is known:

- genre and form;
- poet and textual source, if supplied;
- complete poem, stanza, sher, or isolated line;
- metre (`بحر`) if known;
- rhyme (`قافیہ`) and refrain (`ردیف`);
- required pronunciation and izafat;
- quiet reading, classroom explanation, studio recitation, `تحت اللفظ`, `ترنم`, singing, or mushaira;
- whether repetition or audience response is authorized.

Do not silently complete a misquoted verse. If a line appears corrupt, preserve it and flag the textual question before scanning.

## بحر, وزن, and scansion

Classical Urdu metre is quantitative: it organizes patterns of short and long syllables. It is not English stress metre, and it is not determined by spelling alone.

A practical analysis:

1. normalize the transmitted text without changing words;
2. read it according to plausible Urdu pronunciation;
3. divide it into syllables;
4. mark short and long syllables;
5. account for permitted contraction, combination, or variant pronunciation;
6. compare both lines of a sher with the expected metrical pattern;
7. resolve doubtful izafat or word readings using metre, grammar, meaning, and an authoritative text.

In elementary notation, a short syllable occupies one unit and a long syllable commonly two. Actual recitation is not mechanical counting. The metrical pattern constrains timing while language, syntax, and performance shape surface duration.

Do not “fix” a metre by changing a poet’s word until textual sources and accepted prosodic rules have been checked.

## Syllable length

Spelling can mislead. Follow the pronounced form:

- short vowels generally contribute a short syllable when open;
- a long vowel or closed syllable generally contributes a long syllable;
- word boundaries, consonant clusters, and conventional poetic pronunciation can alter the scan;
- some final vowels or syllables may admit metrical treatment not obvious from ordinary orthography;
- Arabic and Persian loans must be read as established in Urdu, not automatically by a foreign-language template.

Metre may distinguish otherwise ambiguous readings, but it is evidence alongside meaning and grammar—not permission to invent a pronunciation.

## Izafat

Izafat links elements, commonly with an audible `-e-` or `-ye-` relation, as in forms conventionally written with `ِ`, `ۂ`, or `ئے` depending orthography and word ending. In poetry it affects both meaning and metre.

Check:

- whether an izafat relation is grammatically and semantically present;
- how it is written in the supplied edition;
- whether the vowel creates a short syllable or joins the surrounding metrical pattern;
- whether the TTS voice realizes the connection;
- whether a selective diacritic is needed in the provider copy.

Never add izafat merely to make diction sound classical. Do not remove it because a system fails to read it; keep the canonical text and make a separate tested pronunciation adaptation.

## قافیہ and ردیف

In a ghazal:

- `ردیف` is the repeated refrain, when present;
- `قافیہ` is the rhyme pattern immediately preceding it;
- the matla commonly establishes the pattern in both lines;
- later second lines return to the same rhyme/refrain expectation.

Performance should let recognition arrive without hammering the ending. Preserve the exact refrain and the rhyme-bearing pronunciation. A pause inside the qafiya-radif unit can destroy anticipation or sense unless the poem and tradition support it.

Rhyme is not only spelling. Listen for the phonological recurrence in the performed form.

## Ambiguous pronunciation

Resolve a doubtful word with several forms of evidence:

1. authoritative edition or poet’s recording;
2. metre;
3. grammar and izafat;
4. semantic fit;
5. established Urdu pronunciation;
6. rhyme;
7. regional or performance tradition.

Document unresolved alternatives. Use selective diacritics in a pronunciation layer only when they help and have been tested. See [pronunciation](pronunciation.md).

## Where pauses are allowed

The line on the page is not a license to pause anywhere. A good pause respects:

- syntax and semantic phrase;
- metrical flow;
- qafiya and radif;
- word connection and izafat;
- rhetorical turn;
- the relationship between the two `مصرعے`.

Commonly defensible sites include:

- the boundary between the two lines of a sher;
- a syntactic phrase boundary that does not break a construction;
- a deliberate moment before a semantic or rhetorical resolution;
- an authorized repetition point in public performance.

Avoid:

- splitting noun from izafat complement;
- separating a postposition from its phrase;
- breaking compound predicates;
- pausing inside qafiya-radif merely for drama;
- inserting identical pauses at every comma;
- stretching a short syllable until the metre loses shape.

A written full stop, comma, or line break informs the reading but does not alone determine it.

## Quiet reading and mushaira are different

### Quiet or literary reading

Prioritize:

- semantic clarity;
- metrical poise without display;
- natural word connection;
- restraint at the rhyme and refrain;
- enough silence for the image or argument to settle.

The reader may keep a smaller pitch and dynamic range. “Quiet” does not mean flat or whispered.

### Mushaira performance

A mushaira is a public, participatory event. The performer may:

- establish audience attention before the verse;
- repeat a line or part of it;
- delay the expected qafiya/radif;
- respond to appreciation;
- expand pitch, timing, and address;
- move between spoken `تحت اللفظ` and more melodic `ترنم`, depending tradition and performer.

These are performance conventions, not additions to the canonical poem. Audience response, repeated lines, poet introductions, and exclamations belong in a performance plan or event transcript, never silently inside the clean poem.

Do not reduce mushaira performance to “more dramatic.” Public anticipation depends on listeners’ knowledge of form, rhyme, refrain, and the social protocol of the gathering.

## Recitation, chanting, and singing

Urdu poetic performance can range from speech-like recitation to chant and full melody. The same poem can survive across modes, but timing and pitch treatment differ.

- `تحت اللفظ`: speech-oriented declamation with attention to verbal and rhythmic structure.
- `ترنم`: melodic chanting shaped by metre, rhyme, and performer practice.
- sung setting: musical metre and repetition may reshape surface timing.

Do not infer a musical mode from the presence of poetry. Ask or state the chosen mode when it materially affects direction.

Religious verse also carries genre-specific reverence and community practice. Naat, marsiya, hamd, and manqabat are not interchangeable emotional presets. Preserve text and consult qualified readers where pronunciation or devotional convention is consequential.

## TTS workflow for poetry

1. Keep the authoritative clean poem untouched.
2. Prepare a scansion and pronunciation sheet outside the utterance.
3. Mark only defensible phrase or line boundaries.
4. Test one line without controls.
5. Add at most one control variable at a time.
6. Compare quiet and public hypotheses only if the requested mode permits both.
7. Have a reader who knows Urdu prosody judge metre, word connection, qafiya/radif, pronunciation, and meaning.

Most general TTS systems expose no verified Urdu metre control. Emotion tags cannot repair broken scansion. Segmenting every line may help structure, but synthesis boundaries can also distort connection and timing.

## Poetry review

Reject the result if:

- any word of the poem changed without authorization;
- an izafat disappeared or appeared;
- rhyme or refrain pronunciation drifted;
- a pause broke grammar or metre;
- generic sorrow, whispering, or theatricality replaced the poem’s mode;
- audience material entered the clean poem;
- a provider tag was treated as prosodic evidence.

## Evidence boundary

Quantitative metre, scansion practice, rhyme/refrain, and izafat are established features of Urdu poetry. Exact realization varies across genre, school, poet, region, and performer. The quiet-versus-mushaira guidance draws on performance scholarship and documented practice; a specific poem still requires textual and native performance judgment.
