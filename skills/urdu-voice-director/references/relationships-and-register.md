## Relationship is grammatical

Urdu encodes social relation through pronouns, agreement, address terms, requests, particles, and how directly a claim is made. Preserve the source relationship before polishing individual words.

| Form | Common tendency | Do not assume |
|---|---|---|
| `آپ` | respect, distance, formality, affection with deference | that it always means emotional distance |
| `تم` | familiarity, equality, intimacy, controlled confrontation | that it is automatically rude |
| `تُو` | intense intimacy, prayer, poetic address, regional or marked use, contempt in some settings | that it is a casual substitute for `تم` |

These are tendencies, not a universal caste system. Families, regions, generations, institutions, and individuals differ. Preserve what the text establishes.

## Agreement and address audit

Check together:

- pronoun: `آپ` / `تم` / `تُو`;
- verb and auxiliary agreement;
- possessive and adjective agreement;
- imperative/request form;
- kinship or role term;
- `جی`, honorific, title, or name;
- the degree of directness;
- any change across the scene.

A register shift may be the event. Moving from `آپ` to `تم`, dropping `جی`, using a personal name, or replacing a kinship term can show closeness, rupture, defiance, or urgency. Do not normalize the shift away—and do not add one without evidence.

## Age and authority

Age does not determine a voice by itself. Distinguish:

- chronological age;
- role-based authority;
- family hierarchy;
- professional or institutional convention;
- the speaker’s confidence and education;
- the particular relationship.

Do not infer `سر`, `میڈم`, `استاد`, `مولانا`, `ڈاکٹر`, or another title merely because a setting makes it plausible. If the source says “teacher” without a form of address, preserve or mark that gap.

## Kinship terms

Terms such as `امی`, `امّاں`, `ماں`, `ابو`, `ابّا`, `بھائی`, `بھائی جان`, `باجی`, `آپا`, `خالہ`, `پھوپھی`, `چاچا`, and `ماموں` carry family history, region, age, and stance. They are not interchangeable labels.

Keep:

- the exact term when it is supplied;
- affectionate or respectful suffixes;
- a repeated kinship address when it organizes the turn;
- asymmetry when two people address each other differently.

Do not standardize every family into one vocabulary.

## Titles, names, and gender

Preserve titles and forms such as `صاحب`, `صاحبہ`, `جناب`, `محترمہ`, `حضرت`, `مولانا`, `بی بی`, and `جی` unless adaptation is authorized. Do not add gendered agreement when the source leaves gender unresolved. Recast neutrally if natural; otherwise report the ambiguity.

Names may have multiple pronunciations or spellings. Social confidence is not evidence of a preferred pronunciation. See [pronunciation](pronunciation.md).

## Religious wording

Religious expressions can be theological content, devotional formula, greeting, quotation, everyday idiom, grief language, gratitude, oath, or character voice. Preserve:

- the wording and theological proposition;
- divine and prophetic names and honorifics;
- Qur’anic or Arabic quotation exactly when supplied;
- the established level of reverence;
- everyday religious expressions without automatically intensifying them.

Do not:

- convert an ordinary line into a sermon;
- add a verse, hadith, prayer, moral lesson, or motive;
- treat all Arabic or Persian vocabulary as inherently more reverent;
- “simplify” wording in a way that changes doctrine;
- stylize Qur’anic recitation or tajwid without specialist context.

For `بسم اللہ الرحمٰن الرحیم`, `ان شاء اللہ`, `ما شاء اللہ`, `الحمد للہ`, `السلام علیکم`, and similar forms, keep the source’s intended wording. Script normalization is permissible only when it does not change what is being said. Pronunciation or recitation direction may require domain review.

## Code-switching as social evidence

English may mark workplace convention, school life, technology, institutional authority, quotation, humor, or a private relationship. Preserve the established pattern, including a complete English turn. Do not add English as a prestige effect or erase it in the name of linguistic purity.

The same speaker may switch more with one addressee than another. That distribution can be part of the relationship and should survive refinement.

## Register shifts in performance

Direction must not override the linguistic relationship:

- “warm” does not authorize changing `آپ` to `تم`;
- “urgent” does not authorize dropping a title;
- “angry” does not authorize contempt vocabulary;
- “devotional” does not authorize an Arabicized rewrite;
- “childlike” does not authorize grammatical incompetence.

If the performance interpretation requires a social change not present in the words, label it as an authorial option rather than silently applying it.

## Review questions

1. Who can say this exact form to whom?
2. Did any pronoun, agreement, title, or kinship term change?
3. Did code-switching move, disappear, or multiply?
4. Did respect become distance, or familiarity become disrespect?
5. Was religious wording preserved without embellishment?
6. Did a plausible demographic inference become an invented fact?

## Evidence boundary

Urdu honorific agreement and pronoun contrasts are established grammatical and sociolinguistic phenomena. Their interpretation in a particular family or scene is contextual. Guidance on preserving titles, religious wording, and code-switch patterns is an editorial fidelity rule, not a claim that all speakers use them identically.
