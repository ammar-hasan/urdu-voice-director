# Emotional transitions

## Beat-map method

For each turn, identify:

1. **Starting stance** — what the speaker believes or protects.
2. **Trigger** — a word, event, memory, or response that changes something.
3. **Shift** — what becomes newly felt, understood, or intended.
4. **Audible consequence** — pause, restart, emphasis, softer/lower delivery, shorter syntax, interruption, or a sincere question.

Example:

```text
اوہ... یعنی جب کوئی نہ دیکھ رہا ہو، تب بھی یہ غلط ہے؟
```

Beat map:

`casual defense → implication lands → brief processing → reformulation → sincere check`

Directed:

```text
[the defense drops] اوہ...
[after a brief processing beat, sincerely] یعنی جب کوئی نہ دیکھ رہا ہو، تب بھی یہ غلط ہے؟
```

## Common arcs

### Defensiveness to understanding

Do not jump directly to obedient agreement. Let the speaker protect themselves, encounter the new idea, process it, and then ask or concede.

### Teasing to hurt

Let the teasing rhythm break when the speaker realizes the other person is genuinely hurt. Avoid melodrama; a smaller voice or unfinished joke may be enough.

### Suppressed anger to boundary

Keep control audible. Shorter clauses and precise stress may be more credible than volume.

### Embarrassment to recovery

Possible signals include self-correction, a rushed explanation, joking, changed address form, or a small pause before admitting the fact.

### Uncertainty to decision

Let fillers or reformulation narrow toward a firmer final clause. Do not tag the whole sentence `[confident]`.

## Emphasis

Mark contrast-bearing words, not every emotional word:

```text
میں نے یہ نہیں کہا کہ تم *نہیں* آ سکتے۔
```

When typography may leak into TTS or captions, express emphasis in a non-spoken note:

```text
Emphasis: نہیں
```

Do not use Latin capitalization tricks in Urdu.

## Alternative interpretations

When a line plausibly supports two readings but context slightly favors one:

- choose the likely reading;
- mention the alternative in one sentence;
- do not duplicate the whole script unless the user asks for variants.

When the readings materially change the scene, ask.
