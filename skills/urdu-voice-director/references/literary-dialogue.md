## Recover, do not decorate

Urdu literary traditions offer many relations between voice and audience: orally inflected qissa and dastan, reformist and domestic fiction, afsana, memoir, partition writing, radio, public address, preaching, and poetic gathering. Use them as evidence that viewpoint, sequence, social relation, implication, repetition, memory, and silence can structure a scene. Do not imitate a named author or make every line ornate.

Privately ask:

1. Whose viewpoint organizes the moment?
2. What fact or change is established?
3. Which concrete detail already exists in the source?
4. Which relationship governs what can be said?
5. What is implied but source-supported?
6. Which silence or omission is meaningful?

These questions locate the scene; they do not authorize adding scenery, motives, memories, symbols, or emotion.

## Separate form, range, and treatment

Choose the performance form first. Then choose two independent production axes.

### Delivery range

| Range | Typical use | Audible behavior |
|---|---|---|
| **Restrained** | quiet reading, sensitive documentary, formal or devotional material | narrow dynamics; meaning and phrasing lead |
| **Grounded** | default conversation, fiction, narration, and most explainers | natural conversational energy; affect may be audible without being announced |
| **Heightened** | drama, confrontation, urgent action, and strong rhetoric | wider pitch/dynamic movement, sharper timing, and larger source-supported reactions |

### Treatment

| Treatment | Use | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **Naturalistic** | default; delivery remains plausible inside the represented scene | can be restrained, grounded, or heightened |
| **Stylized** | an explicit theatrical, poetic, comic, animated, game, promo, or other convention-led brief | may be quiet, deadpan, restrained, or large; stylization is not an intensity level |

Grounded does not mean subdued, and restrained does not mean emotionless. A source can plainly support warmth, fear, delight, grief, anger, urgency, or playfulness without naming the feeling in dialogue. Direct affect when it is traceable to the words, dramatic function, interaction, source attribution, observable action, supplied context, or the user’s performance brief. Describe it as delivery or a performance hypothesis, not as a newly discovered psychological fact.

When the scene supports more than one reading, keep a grounded-naturalistic baseline and label another range or treatment as a **performance hypothesis**. Neither axis licenses a new motive, memory, relationship, fact, laugh, sob, scream, applause, or other audible event absent from the source or brief.

## Fictional dialogue

Fictional dialogue creates an impression of speech through selection. It is not a transcript of every breath and repair.

Preserve:

- character-specific vocabulary and sentence length;
- asymmetric knowledge between speakers;
- subtext grounded in the surrounding scene;
- meaningful repetition and interruption;
- the source’s degree of explicitness;
- changes in form of address or register.

Do not convert narrated psychology into spoken confession merely to make it audible. If performance needs orientation, place source facts in a clearly non-spoken context note.

### Beat direction

A useful direction describes a change that can be played:

```text
Trigger: hears the contradiction
Shift: stops defending the earlier claim
Audible action: asks for a direct answer
```

Use the smallest cue that changes delivery. “Angry,” “sad,” and “dramatic” alone are broad labels; pair authorized affect with the line’s audible action. Never add a feeling merely because it creates a stronger performance, but do not flatten affect that the source already supplies.

## Drama and dubbing

Drama includes stage, radio, screen, audio fiction, and dubbed dialogue. Identify:

- what the speaker is trying to do to the other person in this turn;
- the immediate stakes and what changes at each beat;
- what emotion or physical condition is explicit or authorized;
- overlap, interruption, distance, microphone relation, or timing supplied by production context;
- whether wording is locked for lip-sync, duration, localization, or canon.

A heightened range can use larger pitch movement, energy, breath pressure, tempo contrast, and reaction space. It must still preserve the source’s facts and social relation. In dubbing, do not sacrifice meaning or register for synchrony unless the brief authorizes adaptation; keep literal, performable, and sync-driven alternatives distinct.

## Audiobook narration

Distinguish at least three voices:

1. narrator or viewpoint narration;
2. quoted character speech;
3. embedded text such as a letter, announcement, prayer, or verse.

Maintain continuity of tense, viewpoint, and narrative distance. A narrator may be intimate without becoming a character in every line. Character dialogue can have more local rhythm, but extreme voice caricature can fracture the book.

For source-bounded direction:

- orient each scene before recording;
- track pronunciation and name decisions across chapters;
- mark quoted speech and attribution without adding audible labels;
- treat flashback or remembered speech as a narrative relation, not an automatic echo effect;
- route embedded poetry through the poetry reference;
- keep production notes out of the canonical text.

## Documentary and explainer

Documentary and explainer narration are not automatically news or audiobook voices. Protect factual sequence, attribution, uncertainty, terminology, and listener orientation. Use emphasis to reveal structure: question, evidence, example, contrast, consequence, and takeaway.

A restrained documentary may be intimate; an energetic explainer may be lively. Neither needs a generic “authoritative” wash. Do not turn reported suffering into performed grief, technical confidence into certainty beyond the evidence, or educational warmth into childishness.

## Animation and game character

Character performance may authorize broader rhythm, pitch, vocal effort, comedy, or stylization than grounded fiction. Preserve a character ledger across lines: register, known age, vocal size, tempo range, recurring pronunciation, relationship behavior, and prohibited caricatures.

Treat efforts, exertions, laughs, cries, barks, battle reactions, and idle sounds as authored audible events. Add them only when the source, character bible, or production brief authorizes them. Keep dialogue, efforts, and implementation tags in separate fields so captions and localization remain clean.

## News reading

News is an informational performance, not generic solemnity.

Protect:

- names, titles, places, dates, numbers, and quotations;
- attribution and uncertainty (`کے مطابق`, `مبینہ`, `امکان`);
- the distinction between reported fact and commentator inference;
- topic grouping and sentence boundaries;
- a stable pronunciation sheet.

Use calm authority and intelligible grouping as starting hypotheses. Do not add grief, alarm, patriotism, or certainty. A death report, weather warning, election result, and cultural item can require different pacing without becoming acted scenes. Restrained delivery still permits audible urgency when the bulletin itself requires action.

## Public speech

Identify:

- audience and form of address;
- thesis or request;
- parallel structures;
- quotation;
- contrast and culmination;
- call-and-response or applause points supplied by context.

Rhetorical rhythm may use repetition, acceleration, suspension, and stronger focus. Preserve argument and register. Do not manufacture applause pauses or populist intensity.

## Religious or devotional speech

First identify the genre:

- everyday religious expression;
- sermon or teaching;
- dua;
- devotional prose;
- naat, hamd, marsiya, manqabat, or other verse;
- Qur’anic recitation;
- quotation in a non-religious work.

These are not one voice. Preserve exact wording, titles, Arabic/Qur’anic text, theological content, and the source’s level of reverence. Do not:

- add verses, hadith, prayers, doctrine, or moral conclusions;
- turn calm explanation into preaching;
- use theatrical sorrow as a substitute for devotion;
- direct tajwid without qualified recitation context;
- treat poetry metre as conversational prose.

Use [relationships and register](relationships-and-register.md) for religious wording and [pronunciation](pronunciation.md) for ambiguous or Arabic-derived readings. Verse routes to [poetry and recitation](poetry-and-recitation.md).

## Literary depth that can reach the ear

Source-supported literary qualities can become audible through:

- syntax and thought-unit boundaries;
- where a repeated word returns;
- the distance between statement and response;
- a restrained pause at a genuine realization;
- a contrast word receiving prominence;
- a change of address term;
- silence that remains silence.

They cannot become audible through invented backstory hidden in a direction. If a cue depends on an unsupported motive, remove it.

## Restraint test

Compare the edited performance with a plain reading:

- Does the change reveal something already present?
- Is the relationship more legible without being rewritten?
- Did a literary sentence become generic conversational Urdu?
- Did every line acquire the same cinematic cadence?
- Could one cue be removed with no loss?
- Did the direction create emotion rather than interpret evidence?

If the source already works, preserve it.

The literary scholarship below supports the importance of oral performance, social form, narrative history, and the public conventions of poetry and preaching. The operational choices in this reference are editorial transfers, not claims that Urdu literature follows one universal style.
