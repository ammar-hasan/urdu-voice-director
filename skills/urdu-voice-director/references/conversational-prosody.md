## Read tendencies, not punctuation recipes

Urdu prosody varies with speaker, dialect, syntax, information structure, emotion, genre, and recording situation. Controlled studies and spontaneous speech do not always produce the same pattern. Use the findings below to form a hypothesis, then listen.

Commonly reported tendencies include:

- statements often end with a fall;
- many `wh`-questions also end with a fall rather than an English-like generic question rise;
- yes/no questions commonly use a final rise;
- contrastive or corrective focus can use a larger pitch movement and longer duration on the focused word;
- material after a focused word may become less prominent through pitch-range compression;
- individual speakers may realize the same focus or sentence type differently.

None of these is absolute. Lexical content, word order, focus position, phrase boundaries, and speaker habit can change the realization.

## Statements and questions

Do not convert punctuation directly into melody.

```text
وہ کب آیا؟
```

is a `wh`-question, but the question mark alone does not require a final rise. Conversely:

```text
وہ آ گیا؟
```

often invites a rising yes/no contour, yet surprise, confirmation, disbelief, or echo reading can reshape it.

Use the conversational function:

- requesting new information;
- confirming a proposition;
- echoing what was heard;
- challenging an assumption;
- asking rhetorically;
- leaving a turn open.

Then test the selected voice or actor rather than prescribing a categorical arrow.

## Focus and prominence

Contrastive focus answers an implicit alternative:

```text
میں نے چائے مانگی تھی، کافی نہیں۔
```

The focused item may receive:

- a wider pitch excursion;
- a higher or later peak;
- longer duration;
- clearer articulation;
- reduced prominence after it.

Do not stack all of these controls. One usable direction such as “make `چائے` the correction; let the rest settle” is often better than a technical contour. If the TTS model exposes no verified focus control, keep the note outside the utterance and try wording or segmentation only when meaning remains intact.

Post-focus compression is a useful hypothesis, not a universal Urdu rule. Experimental results vary with speaker and syntactic configuration.

## Prosodic phrasing

Speech groups words into accentual and intonational phrases that do not always match commas or line breaks. A phrase boundary may be motivated by:

- syntax;
- information grouping;
- breath;
- a turn-management need;
- a change in intention;
- a deliberate rhetorical or poetic boundary.

Punctuation supports reading but does not reliably define performance. An ellipsis can represent omission, trailing away, hesitation, or simply a typographic habit. A dash can represent interruption, self-repair, apposition, or emphasis. Diagnose the event before choosing the mark or cue.

## Performance modes

### Everyday conversation

Let response structure and relationship carry most of the prosody. Avoid directing every content word.

### Fictional dialogue

Use prosody to reveal source-supported subtext or a beat change, not to add invisible psychology. Designed dialogue may be cleaner than spontaneous conversation.

### News

Prefer informational grouping, stable names and numbers, and restrained prominence. A headline, quoted speech, and a developing report may need different grouping, but “serious” is not a complete direction.

### Public and devotional speech

Rhetorical parallelism, audience address, quotation, and conclusion may expand phrase-final movement or timing. Preserve the genre: devotional reverence is not the same as theatrical solemnity.

### Poetry

Do not apply conversational contours before checking metre, rhyme, refrain, izafat, and semantic phrasing. Load [poetry and recitation](poetry-and-recitation.md).

## Direction that survives contact with performance

Prefer:

```text
Make “اب” the correction; release the rest of the sentence.
```

over:

```text
Raise pitch 30 Hz, lengthen 18%, then compress the post-focus range.
```

The second describes a possible analysis, not a portable acting direction. Precise acoustic manipulation is appropriate only in a system that actually supports and has tested those controls.

## Listening protocol

For an important line, render or rehearse at least:

1. a neutral reading;
2. one focused hypothesis;
3. one restrained alternative.

Listen blind for:

- sentence type;
- the word heard as important;
- whether focus changes the proposition;
- unnatural singsong;
- post-focus words becoming inaudible;
- a punctuation-induced pause that breaks grammar;
- consistency with the relationship and genre.

Record the exact voice/model and keep the clean text constant when comparing a delivery control.

## Evidence boundary

The broad statement/question and focus patterns are established tendencies in the cited Urdu studies. Their strength and consistency differ across controlled and spontaneous data. Directions derived from them are hypotheses until a native listener evaluates the actual performance.
