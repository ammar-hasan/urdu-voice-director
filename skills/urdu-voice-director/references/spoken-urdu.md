## The target is designed speech

Natural dialogue is not a stenographic transcript. It selects enough fragments, particles, repetition, response forms, and hesitation to create credible online speech while removing noise that does no dramatic or communicative work. This is especially important in fiction and educational material: polish should not erase speech, but “naturalness” should not become clutter.

Use these editorial tests:

- Can the line be spoken in one plausible sequence of thought units?
- Does each clause respond to what was just said?
- Would this person use these words with this listener?
- Does a repetition, fragment, or particle reveal stance, repair, insistence, or turn-taking?
- Would removing it make the line cleaner without making it less alive?

Keep what earns its place.

## Thought units

Written Urdu often packs cause, contrast, condition, and conclusion into one sentence. Spoken Urdu commonly distributes them:

```text
Written:
مجھے اس فیصلے پر اعتراض ہے کیونکہ اس سے مسئلہ حل ہونے کے بجائے مزید پیچیدہ ہو جائے گا۔

Possible spoken realization:
مجھے اس فیصلے پر اعتراض ہے۔ اس سے مسئلہ حل نہیں ہوگا—اور پیچیدہ ہو جائے گا۔
```

The second version is not automatically better. Use it only if the situation calls for direct speech and the wording preserves the same claim. Do not cut clauses so aggressively that reasoning becomes childish or breathless.

## Particles and response forms

Items such as `ارے`, `اچھا`, `اوہ`, `تو`, `بھئی`, `ہاں`, `نہیں`, `بس`, `دیکھو`, `سنو`, `رُکو`, `چلو`, `جی`, and `نا` can organize turns, mark stance, soften or sharpen a response, acknowledge news, or claim the floor. They are not generic “natural Urdu” decoration.

Before adding one, ask:

1. What conversational job does it do?
2. Would this speaker use it with this addressee?
3. Does the source support the stance it introduces?

If those answers are weak, omit it.

## Repetition, fragments, and repair

Repetition may signal disbelief, insistence, self-correction, memory, or an attempt to hold the floor:

```text
ذمہ داری؟ تم اسے ذمہ داری کہتے ہو؟
```

A fragment can be more credible than a completed sentence when the other speaker supplies the missing context:

```text
اگر وہ واپس نہ آیا تو؟
```

Do not “repair” every incomplete construction into textbook prose. Conversely, do not add stammers or repeated words merely to make a line look spontaneous. See [hesitation and pauses](hesitation-and-pauses.md) when the interruption itself matters.

## Children and family speech

Children can be precise, perceptive, devout, playful, or formal. Age-appropriate does not mean foolish. A useful default is:

- shorter chains of reasoning;
- more concrete reference;
- one discovery or objection at a time;
- vocabulary established by family, school, media, or the source;
- no adult essay inserted to deliver a lesson.

Parents and teachers need not become sermon voices. Preserve the lesson the source actually contains; do not add a moral conclusion for neatness.

## Roman Urdu

Roman Urdu is a writing practice, not a stable transliteration standard. Recover the utterance from context rather than converting letter by letter.

```text
kal
```

may mean `کل` as “yesterday” or “tomorrow”; the sentence and scene decide. Names, regional forms, English words, and `ہے/ہیں`, `کیا/کِیا`, or gendered agreement may remain ambiguous. Do not silently resolve them.

When script conversion is requested:

1. reconstruct the relationship and topic;
2. identify ambiguous words, names, and agreement;
3. preserve genuine English turns or terms;
4. produce Urdu script;
5. list only ambiguities that materially affect the result.

## Code-switching

Code-switching may mark profession, education, platform, intimacy, irony, quotation, or the lexical norm of a domain. Preserve it when it belongs to the person or setting.

Do not:

- add English to make a character sound modern;
- translate an ordinary established term merely to maximize Urdu purity;
- replace a complete English turn that is clearly part of the character’s voice;
- assume every English token needs English phonology in synthesis.

Script choice and pronunciation choice are separate. See [pronunciation](pronunciation.md) for mixed-script testing and [relationships and register](relationships-and-register.md) when switching carries identity or social distance.

## Read-aloud check

Read the clean text aloud once without performance notes. Listen for:

- breathless clause chains;
- repeated information rather than meaningful repetition;
- particles that do no work;
- register shifts;
- translated word order;
- captions that accidentally contain directions;
- a line improved only because it is different.

An unchanged source line is a successful outcome when it already speaks well.

## Evidence boundary

The distinction between fictional orality and raw transcription is research-supported. The particular wording changes above are editorial decisions and remain context-sensitive. Roman Urdu variability and Urdu–English switching are well documented, but no single corpus defines every community’s norm.
