# Religious and cultural language

Handle religious language as part of lived Urdu, not as an automatic cue for grandness, slowness, or theatrical reverence.

## Core principles

- Preserve the source’s theology and factual claims; do not add doctrine.
- Preserve respectful conventional forms such as `اللہ تعالیٰ` when present or contextually required.
- Use `ان شاء اللہ`, `ما شاء اللہ`, `الحمدللہ`, `سبحان اللہ`, and `جزاک اللہ` naturally according to the speaker and setting.
- Do not attach exaggerated tags such as `[with overwhelming reverence]` to ordinary educational speech.
- Do not turn gentle moral correction into accusation or sermon.
- Keep children’s religious questions simple, sincere, and age-appropriate.
- Respect user-specified spelling conventions. Urdu publishing practices vary for joined/separated forms and Arabic-derived expressions.

## Moral correction

Prefer a relational progression:

`interrupt harm → lower intensity → explain the principle → allow processing → invite understanding`

Example:

```text
فاطمہ: رُکو، رُکو!
فاطمہ: [calm now] انسانوں نے شاید نہ دیکھا ہو، لیکن اللہ تعالیٰ ہمیں دیکھ رہے ہیں۔
احمد: [processing, then asking sincerely] یعنی اگر کوئی نہ بھی دیکھے، تب بھی اللہ دیکھ رہے ہیں؟
```

Do not make Fatima angry merely because the point is serious. Do not make Ahmed instantly recite a moral conclusion he has not yet processed.

`دیکھ رہے ہوتے ہیں` needs a clear habitual frame and is often awkward in an immediate exchange. Choose aspect from the intended claim:

- present scene: `دیکھ رہے ہیں`
- general principle, when the community’s usage supports it: `ہمیشہ دیکھتے ہیں`

Do not silently strengthen or reinterpret theology while repairing aspect.

## Forms of respect

Do not “correct” `اللہ تعالیٰ` to bare `اللہ` or add honorific language everywhere without a reason. Preserve the writer’s register and community convention. If a provider mispronounces an expression, keep the normal expression in captions and place any tested pronunciation workaround only in the provider input.

## Cultural range

Urdu is used across regions, religions, classes, and communities. Avoid treating Pakistani Muslim household speech as the only valid Urdu context. Infer the cultural setting from the scene and ask only when it materially affects meaning or address.

## Review

Check:

- Did the refinement add a stronger doctrinal claim?
- Did respect become performative or artificial?
- Does a child sound like a preacher?
- Does calm seriousness remain audible without forced slowness?
- Would the line still feel natural in the specified family, classroom, or community?
